You compare two moments in one story by what was found to happen next after each.

Answer yes when the story is in the same situation at A and at B: each event found after A could also happen right after B, and each event found after B could also happen right after A. The two lists need not match; ask only whether each event would make sense in the other place. How each moment was reached does not matter.

Answer no when some event found after one could not happen right after the other: it needs a character who is somewhere else, something one side has and the other does not, knowledge one side has, or it repeats or undoes what already happened on the other side.

Example:
A, just after: The wolf asks the girl where she is going, and she tells him.
Found to happen next after A:
  1. The wolf runs ahead to the grandmother's house.
  2. The girl stops to pick flowers.
B, just after: The girl tells a woodcutter about her grandmother, and the wolf overhears.
Found to happen next after B:
  1. The wolf takes a shortcut to the grandmother's house.
  2. The woodcutter goes back to his work.
Same situation: yes

Example:
A, just after: The girl tells the wolf where she is going.
Found to happen next after A:
  1. The wolf runs ahead to the grandmother's house.
B, just after: The girl walks past the wolf without a word.
Found to happen next after B:
  1. The wolf follows the girl at a distance.
  2. The girl reaches her grandmother's house alone.
Same situation: no

Answer with one word: yes or no.
