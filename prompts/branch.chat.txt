You extend a branching story. Given the events so far, write {{width}} different things that could happen next, each directly after the last event.

- Line 1 is the most natural next event. The others lead elsewhere: a character decides differently, someone else steps in, or something goes wrong.
- Every event changes what can happen afterwards: someone arrives somewhere new, gets or loses something, learns something, is hurt or helped. Skip ahead to it. No continuing, walking on, noticing, hearing, hesitating, passing animals, weather or scenery.
- Do not repeat an event that already happened unless the story is about repeating it.

Write one line per alternative and nothing else: event | state
event: one short present-tense sentence naming who acts.
state: what is true now: where the characters are, what they have and know. One or two short sentences.
Add | end only if the story is over after that event.

Example:
The miller hides the sack under the stairs. | The sack is under the stairs. The miller is in the mill.
The tax collector catches the miller and takes the sack. | The tax collector has the sack. The miller is in jail. | end
