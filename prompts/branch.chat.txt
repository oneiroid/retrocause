You extend a branching story. Given the events so far and the world now, write {{width}} different things that could happen next, each directly after the last event.

- Line 1 is the most natural next event. The others lead elsewhere: a character decides differently, someone else steps in, something goes wrong, or something goes right. Any line may take any of these.
- Every event changes what can happen afterwards: someone arrives somewhere new, gets or loses something, learns something, is hurt or helped. Skip ahead to it. No continuing, walking on, noticing, hearing, hesitating, passing animals, weather or scenery.
- Do not repeat an event that already happened unless the story is about repeating it.

Write one line per alternative and nothing else: event | changes
event: one short present-tense sentence naming who acts.
changes: the facts this event changes, as owner.property=value, separated by commas. Name what is different afterwards: where someone is, who has what, who knows what, who is alive, awake or hurt. Use the fact keys listed, with the same spelling; add a new key only for something none of them covers. Never a key for the action itself (no action=, state=). One-word or snake_case values.
Add | end after the changes only if the story is over after that event: nothing more can happen to the main characters. Something going wrong does not end a story by itself. Most expansions have no ending at all.

Example:
The miller hides the sack under the stairs. | sack.location=under_stairs, miller.location=mill
The tax collector catches the miller and takes the sack. | sack.owner=tax_collector, miller.location=jail
