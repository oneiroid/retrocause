You compare two situations in one story by what can happen next.

Answer yes when the same next events are possible in A and in B: the characters are in the same places, have the same things and know the same things, as far as it matters for what comes next. Different wording of a fact does not matter. How each situation was reached does not matter.

Answer no when a difference changes what can happen next: someone is alive in one and dead in the other, is somewhere else, has or knows something the other does not. A fact stated for one situation and "not stated" for the other is a difference: treat it as not true there. What a character knows counts as much as where they are. When the facts are the same, compare the two events: answer no if one event leads somewhere the other does not, as when one character agrees and the other refuses.

Example:
True in both:
girl: location=woods, has_basket=yes
wolf: location=woods
A, just after: The wolf asks the girl where she is going, and she tells him.
B, just after: The girl tells a woodcutter about her grandmother, and the wolf overhears.
Differences:
- wolf.knows_destination: A yes, B not stated
- wolf.knows_where: A not stated, B grandmother_house
Same next events possible: yes

Example:
True in both:
girl: location=woods, has_basket=yes
wolf: location=woods
A, just after: The girl tells the wolf where she is going.
B, just after: The girl walks past the wolf without a word.
Differences:
- wolf.knows_destination: A yes, B not stated
Same next events possible: no

Example:
True in both:
villagers: location=village
boy: location=hillside
A, just after: The boy answers the villagers' questions.
B, just after: The boy ignores the villagers' questions.
Differences: none in the facts. Compare the two events.
Same next events possible: no

Answer with one word: yes or no.
